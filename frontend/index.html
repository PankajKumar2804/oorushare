01 · The Ground Reality

Why 10-Minute Delivery Broke Bengaluru's Residential Bylanes

Bengaluru experiences 2.5 million quick-commerce deliveries every single day. While hyper-efficient for consumers, 30-foot residential cross roads in HSR Layout, Indiranagar, and Koramangala were never engineered to absorb delivery surges without dedicated curb space.

The Resident's Grievance

Delivery bikes park across compound gates for "just 2 minutes", blocking residents from leaving for work. Neighborhood WhatsApp groups are filled with gate-blocking disputes and heated confrontations.

88% of RWA disputes cite delivery obstruction

The Gig Worker's Penalty

Delivery riders face ruthless 10-minute delivery timers. With nowhere legal to stop, they risk ₹1,000 towing fines from Bengaluru Traffic Police (BTP) and unsafe parking hazards on narrow pavements.

₹1,500/month average fines per rider

The City's Environmental Cost

Coupled with two-wheeler idling during drop-offs, choked bylanes slow down entire arterial networks, delaying emergency vehicles and generating over 11 tonnes of CO₂ emissions daily.

140,000 transit hours lost daily citywide
02 · The Architecture

The 4-Sided Civic Curb Network

OoruShare creates a self-balancing cooperative curb economy connecting four previously-uncoordinated actors: residents, gig riders, dark store dispatchers, and RWA neighborhood associations.

Component A · Residential Ledger
Driveway Sharing with RWA Maintenance Offsets

Residents list their empty driveway gates during office hours (e.g., 11:00 AM – 3:00 PM). Each 2-minute authorized delivery drop awards 10 community credits to the resident's account. Credits convert directly into ₹50 discounts on monthly society maintenance bills, certified by the local RWA.

Component B · Spatial Radar
Rider Arrives at a Legal Bay — No New App

Via BAA dispatch integration, riders receive the pre-allocated bay coordinate inside their existing delivery app before they leave the dark store. The spatial radar is a fallback for new or cross-zone riders — experienced local riders already know their spots. What they can't solve individually is dispatch clustering.

03 · Autonomous AI Layer

The Autonomous Curb Governor (Built with IBM Bob)

Unlike static reservation apps, OoruShare operates as an autonomous, self-balancing feedback loop powered by IBM Granite 3.8B time-series congestion forecasting and watsonx.governance explainability.

Perpetual Agent Control Loop

Perception → Reasoning → Action → Self-Correction

Phase 1 · Perception
Ingests Dispatches & Sensors

Monitors active quick-commerce dispatches, GPS coordinates, and historical bylane saturation in HSR Layout and Indiranagar.

Phase 2 · Reasoning
IBM Granite 3.8B Forecasting

Predicts 15-minute curb saturation spikes and calculates bylane width limits to ensure emergency vehicle clearance.

Phase 3 · Action
Dynamic Bay Allocation

Locks the optimal driveway bay via PostgreSQL advisory locks and signals the dark store to throttle dispatch intervals.

Phase 4 · Reflection
Autonomous Conflict Reroute

If a resident's car blocks the bay, the agent detects the conflict and re-allocates the rider to the nearest alternative bay in <500ms.
